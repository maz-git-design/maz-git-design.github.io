I am a Research Assistant at Newcastle University, United Kingdom, working on energy-efficient computing architectures for artificial intelligence, with a particular focus on in-memory computing and integrated circuit design.

I hold an MSc in Microelectronics: Systems and Devices from Newcastle University, where I studied as a Chevening Scholar, and an Engineering degree in Electronics and Computer Science from the University of Kinshasa, Democratic Republic of the Congo.

My research focuses on developing energy-efficient hardware architectures for intelligent systems by exploring the intersection of microelectronics, computer architecture, and machine learning. I am particularly interested in memory-centric computing architectures that reduce data movement and enable efficient on-chip learning.

My recent research investigates SRAM-based in-memory computing architectures for Tsetlin Machines, with an emphasis on hardware-efficient state updates, circuit-level design, and low-power implementation.

My broader research interests include VLSI design, emerging memory technologies, digital and mixed-signal integrated circuits, FPGA-based acceleration, and Edge AI.

Alongside my research, I have experience in university teaching, embedded systems, digital hardware design, and software engineering.

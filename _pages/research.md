My research focuses on energy-efficient computing architectures at the intersection of microelectronics, computer architecture, and machine learning. I am particularly interested in memory-centric hardware systems that reduce data movement and support efficient artificial intelligence computation.

## In-Memory Computing

My current work investigates **Design and Simulation of a Training-Aware SRAM-Based In-Memory Macro for Tsetlin Automata State Updates**. The investigation examines circuit-level architectures and mechanisms for efficient state updates in SRAM-based computing systems.

The focus includes SRAM-based in-memory computing, training-aware memory architectures, hardware-efficient state updates, and Tsetlin Machine hardware. On-chip learning and memory-centric computing architectures provide the broader research context.

[Read about the SRAM-based computing project]({{ '/projects/sram-in-memory-computing/' | relative_url }}).

## Energy-Efficient AI Hardware

My broader research interests include Edge AI accelerators, hardware-aware machine learning, FPGA-based acceleration, quantized neural networks, low-power digital architectures, and hardware/software co-design.

These interests guide potential future investigations; they are not presented as completed accelerator projects or published contributions.

## Emerging Memory Technologies

I am interested in FeFET and ferroelectric memories, gain-cell eDRAM, emerging nonvolatile memory technologies, memory-based computation, and differential memory architectures.

These are exploratory research directions, distinct from my current SRAM-based work.
